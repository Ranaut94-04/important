## 1. Final video editing plan (the strategy)

**Objective:** Stop the scroll in <2s, hold retention across 30s, convert to a follow.
**Core idea:** "Thread → creation → keepsake." We open on raw craft (macro yarn), bloom into finished products, and close on the emotional promise (memories that last). Every cut is motivated by the VO.

**Why it works (retention logic):**
- **Hook = curiosity + texture**, not a product. A face or a logo up front is skippable; an unresolved *"what is this becoming?"* macro isn't. Payoff arrives at ~2.3s (the reveal), rewarding the pause.
- **Pace ramps.** Scenes 1–4 breathe (~3s each with slow internal pushes). Scenes 5–8 quicken (pops, dissolves) so energy *rises* into the CTA — the opposite of a slideshow that flatlines.
- **The luxury feel is 80% motion + grade, 20% content.** Smooth eased camera moves, consistent warm grade, and subtle bloom/halation do more than any single "expensive" shot.

**The honest pacing trade-off:** 8 scenes in ~23s = ~2.9s/scene. That is *punchy*, not *slow*. You cannot have both. My recommendation: keep 8 scenes but let the motion inside each shot be slow and eased (a 4% push over 3s reads as "calm luxury" even on a short cut). If you want true "let-it-breathe" slowness, drop to 6 hero scenes and give each ~4s — say the word and I'll re-cut the timeline.

## 8. Editing notes (the craft details)

- **Motion easing is everything.** Every camera move uses ease-in-*and*-out (Premiere: F9 → Ease; DaVinci: right-click keyframe → Ease In/Out). Linear moves read cheap and robotic. Never let a still just sit — a 3–5% push/pull over its duration keeps it alive.
- **Consistent push direction per section** feels intentional; random zoom directions feel like a slideshow.
- **2.5D depth on stills (your "3D camera move"):** separate subject from background (Photoshop cutout or an AI depth tool like Runway/Immersity/LeiaPix), then animate foreground and background at different speeds → real parallax. This is what makes a *photo* feel *filmed*.
- **Transitions — use sparingly and consistently.** Whip-pan (directional blur + fast move) for energy beats; cross-dissolve for calm; light-leak dissolves for warmth. Avoid built-in "cube/star/page-peel" cheese entirely.
- **Overlays (Screen blend mode):** light leaks, dust particles, soft bokeh — grab free packs from Mixkit / Motion Array / Pexels. Keep them subtle (opacity 15–35%). Dust particles especially sell "cozy/dreamy."
- **Whites are the whole brand.** Crochet white can read clinical-blue. Warm it toward ivory in the grade (see §9). Cream, not white, is the mood.
- **Text safe zone:** keep all text within ~10% margins and clear of the bottom ~15% (IG UI covers it).
- **First frame ≠ cover.** Set your reel cover deliberately (§5); don't let IG pick frame 1 (a blurry macro).
- **fps note:** 60fps is fine and keeps moves buttery. If you want a more filmic feel, 24 or 30fps also works — 60fps mainly benefits smooth camera motion here since the sources are stills.

## 9. Color grading settings (the "cozy-premium" look)

Target: warm, soft, lifted, dreamy — think low-contrast pastel film (Kodak Portra vibe), *not* punchy/HDR.

- **White balance:** warm. Temp ~ +8 to +12, Tint ~ +3 (toward magenta, subtle).
- **Contrast:** *reduce* slightly. Lift the toe (matte blacks) — do NOT crush to pure black. Highlights pulled back a touch to avoid clipping the whites.
- **Tone curve:** gentle S with a *raised* black point (soft/faded look).
- **Lift (shadows):** +0.015 to +0.03, nudged warm.
- **Gamma (mids):** slight +, warm.
- **Gain (highlights):** −0.02 to −0.05, warm-neutral.
- **Saturation:** modest global (+5 to +8). Then selectively: **boost yellows/oranges** (sunflowers glow) and **tame reds** — the crochet roses can go garish/electric; pull red saturation down ~10–15% and protect skin tones.
- **HSL qualifier:** select the crochet whites → warm them toward ivory. Select skin (if human shots) → keep natural, avoid over-warming.
- **Finishing:** subtle highlight **bloom/glow** + light **halation** (orange fringe on bright edges) + very light **film grain** + soft **vignette** (warm, low strength). These four together = the "expensive" softness.
- **Delivery:** master **SDR Rec.709** (see HDR warning up top). If you insist on HDR, test on multiple phones before publishing.

## 13. Export settings

- **Container/codec:** MP4, **H.264** (safest for IG; avoids upload quirks). H.265 OK but not necessary.
- **Resolution:** **2160×3840** (vertical 4K — oversampled for sharpness after IG recompression) *or* 1080×1920 at max bitrate. IG delivers reels at 1080×1920 regardless; 2160×3840 source just survives recompression better.
- **Frame rate:** 60fps (match your sequence).
- **Bitrate:** VBR, 2-pass, **target ~25–30 Mbps** (go high — IG re-encodes and strips quality).
- **Keyframe interval:** every 60 frames.
- **Color:** Rec.709, SDR (see HDR warning).
- **Audio:** AAC, 320kbps, 48kHz, stereo, ~−14 to −16 LUFS.
- **Duration:** exactly 30.0s. Reels allow up to 90s but 30s is a strong retention length for this content.

## 15. Premiere Pro editing sequence

**Sequence setup:** New Sequence → Custom → **2160×3840**, 60fps, square pixels, Rec.709.

**Track layout:**
- **V1** — base footage / animated stills
- **V2** — overlays: light leaks, dust, bokeh (blend: **Screen**)
- **V3** — text / scene titles
- **V4** — logo & end-card graphics
- **Adjustment Layer** (top) — global Lumetri grade for the whole reel
- **A1** — VO · **A2** — music bed · **A3** — SFX

**Build order:**
1. Lay all animated clips on V1 per §2 timeline. Add **Transform** keyframes for any extra push/pan (uncheck "Use Composition Shutter Angle," enable Motion Blur if desired). F9 to ease all keyframes.
2. Transitions: whip-pan = **Directional Blur** + a fast Transform move across the cut (or Film Impact "Whip"). Calm cuts = **Cross Dissolve**. Warm cuts = a light-leak clip on V2 dissolving over the cut.
3. Blur reveals = **Gaussian Blur** keyframed high→0.
4. Text on V3/V4 with **Essential Graphics** (fade + scale, eased). Use the fonts in §12.
5. Drop the **.srt** onto the timeline → style the caption track.
6. Add the **Adjustment Layer** → **Lumetri Color** with §9 settings (Basic → Curves → HSL → Creative for bloom/vignette). Add **Lumetri "Vignette"** + a light grain overlay.
7. Audio: VO on A1; music on A2 ducked −8dB under VO (keyframe or use "Auto Ducking" in Essential Sound); SFX on A3 per §11.
8. Export per §13 (Media Encoder → H.264, match settings, bump bitrate).

## 16. DaVinci Resolve node suggestions

Timeline-level: put the whole reel look on a **timeline node / adjustment clip**; per-clip fixes on individual clips.

**Serial node tree (per hero clip or on the adjustment clip):**
- **Node 1 — Balance:** exposure + white balance (warm, §9 temp/tint).
- **Node 2 — Primary grade:** Lift/Gamma/Gain + Curves (matte lifted look).
- **Node 3 — HSL qualifier:** select reds → desaturate ~12%; separate node/qualifier → warm the crochet whites to ivory.
- **Node 4 — Bloom/Glow:** parallel node, blur highlights, composite **Screen** (or use the "Glow" OFX) — low strength.
- **Node 5 — Halation:** parallel node → isolate highlights → blur → tint orange → composite Add/Screen at low opacity.
- **Node 6 — Grain:** **Film Grain** OFX, fine, low strength.
- **Node 7 — Vignette:** soft circular **Power Window**, darken edges slightly, warm.
- **Node 8 — Glamour/soft-focus (faces only):** subtle, optional.

**Fairlight (audio):** VO track → EQ (HPF 80Hz, presence lift ~3kHz), Compressor, De-Esser; music track → sidechain-duck to VO; SFX bus low. Master limiter to −14 LUFS.
